# RapidSim 06 · Do We Switch?

A ten-minute decision plus the debrief. Individual or team.

## The situation

A regional retailer's card payments are down, and every minute costs sales at the registers. The company paid for a backup arrangement for exactly this kind of day. Your students decide whether to fail over to it now or hold where they are. They have a briefing pack, a clock and a store network waiting on them.

## Why it's hard

Switching feels responsible, because that's what the backup is for. Holding feels like standing still while the registers are down. Everything students need for a sound call is in the briefing, fine print included, alongside plenty that's beside the point. Holding takes a one-line reason, and a clock that runs out is recorded as no decision.

## What students take away

Students learn what a backup actually buys and how to check before trusting it: read the arrangement, then the label. They also practise committing to a call with a reason attached, a habit worth more than any single right answer.

## Running it

One ten-minute clock runs for the whole class. In team play the first member to press decides for the team, which starts a good conversation by itself. The Live screen, opened from the session bar, shows the class's decisions on the projector. The debrief walks back through the briefing together, line by line. It fits a unit on IT infrastructure, risk or business continuity. Who in your students' organisations will have read the fine print on the backup?
