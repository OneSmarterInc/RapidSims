# RapidSim+ 02 · Who Pays for the Wire?

Extended format, about sixty-five minutes of play. Teams of four, face to face. Graduate level (MBA, MS).

## The situation

New data centres want electricity, and a utility has begun a $1.2 billion transmission build to serve them. If the demand never arrives, the wire still has to be paid for. Each team of four takes the four seats in a regulatory proceeding: the utility, a data-centre developer, industrial energy users and a consumer advocate. Each seat has its own named person and a private brief with a floor it can't cross.

## Why it's hard

The argument happens out loud, the way a settlement conference does. The software holds a shared term sheet with five terms and a running log, and a term counts only when all four seats sign it. The money terms lock together. Anything left unsigned at the deadline is set by the Commission, and each open term extends a connection freeze that costs every seat.

## What students take away

Students learn how contract terms move risk between parties, that a deal needs something for every seat, and how a known fallback changes the bargaining. The debrief plays each team's terms forward into two futures, demand arriving and demand missing, and ends with the real proceeding behind the sim.

## Running it

Students read an untimed walkthrough, then you split the class into teams of four on the Teams tab; extra students share a seat as co-counsel. You move the session from the session bar: briefing, opening statements, negotiation, with ten more minutes once if needed. Staff notices in the instructor notes nudge a stalled team. The Live screen shows each team's progress, and Rehearse uses four practice students, one per seat. It fits a graduate unit on negotiation, energy policy, regulation or investment under uncertainty. What would your students give up to avoid the Commission's version?
