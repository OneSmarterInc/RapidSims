# RapidSim 02 · What Did It Tell Them?

Twenty minutes of play, then the debrief. Each student plays on their own device; general business background is all it takes.

## The situation

Calder Sealing Systems makes industrial seals, and its customers ask a support assistant called Relay for specifications. Six weeks ago Relay gave a customer a figure from a superseded datasheet, the parts went into service, and they've been running ever since. Your students are the VP of Customer Operations, and this morning somebody noticed.

## Why it's hard

Two competent people are exposed in opposite directions. The director of technical publications owns the documents Relay reads; the head of customer platforms owns how Relay was set up. Each is right about their own part, so each holds their ground. A vendor representative and a junior support engineer fill out the call, and the chief commercial officer weighs in from above. A quick cleanup is on offer, and its cost is something students have to find for themselves.

## What students take away

It's an AI incident, and the lesson reaches past AI: what to preserve before you fix something, and how responsibility splits when an answer comes from several people's decisions at once. The story branches on what students do early, so the debrief can put very different third acts side by side.

## Running it

Students question the characters directly while the clock runs and lock their reading at set points. Results show each student's choices and where they ended up, and the class debrief runs from your screen. It sits beside a unit on AI governance, customer operations or information quality, or stands alone. Plan on an hour with the debrief. When an AI gives a wrong answer, whose answer was it?
