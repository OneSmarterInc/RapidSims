# RapidSim 08 · Eighteen Months Later

A timed selection round, a reveal and the debrief. Best played in teams.

## The situation

A veterinary practice with several clinics needs a new scheduling system. Three vendors are on the shortlist, and all three look sound on paper. Students put six questions to the vendors' representatives before the clock runs out, and every question goes to all three. Then they pick a vendor and commit to a go-live plan.

## Why it's hard

Six questions go fast, and most students spend the first few on features and price because a demo invites it. The vendors answer what they're asked. Some things about each option surface only with the right question, and some come from putting two partial answers together. The go-live plan shapes what happens next as much as the product does.

## What students take away

The reveal jumps eighteen months ahead and shows how adoption went at the clinics. Students see that a system's success depends on what they asked before buying and how they rolled it out, as well as which vendor they chose, and they learn how to spend a limited number of questions in any evaluation.

## Running it

Teams share their six questions, vote privately on the vendor, and the majority decides. You can pause the clock and open the next part from the session bar. Results show each team's outcome and plan, and the debrief shows outcomes before the questions that led to them. Each student gets a short written account of their own outcome. It fits a unit on systems acquisition, implementation or vendor management. What would your students ask first if they had only six?
