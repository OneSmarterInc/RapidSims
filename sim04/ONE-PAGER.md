# RapidSim 04 · Whose Number Is Right?

About twenty-five minutes. Individual or team, any number of teams, even one.

## The situation

Ridgeway Dispatch sells scheduling and invoicing software to trade businesses on three subscription tiers. The board wants one number: customer retention for the year. Each student or team gets the same forty-account roster and a reporting guide, calculates the figure and commits to it. Then the whole class sees every number at once.

## Why it's hard

The arithmetic is quick, and students who are good with spreadsheets finish fast. The hard part comes after they commit, when they explain their number to a class that may hold a different one. Students expect careful people with shared data to agree, and the sim puts that expectation in front of everyone.

## What students take away

A business metric is a decision someone made before it's a number anyone calculates. Students leave able to ask the right question whenever two reports disagree, before arguing about whose spreadsheet is wrong. It's short, and it changes how people read a dashboard.

## Running it

Students join from the class link; you choose team or individual play, split the class with Auto split, then start the clock. Results show each number against its own working sheet. The debrief shows every number, the two furthest apart, the definitions behind them and a worked calculation, and you open the review questions when you're ready. It fits a unit on data, metrics or business intelligence, and it's short enough to open a class on something else. How many numbers in your own program's reports would pass the same test?
