# RapidSim 09 · Where Does the Money Land?

A statement, an allocation round, a staged reveal and the debrief. Individual or team.

## The situation

It's June 2000, and your students manage a fund. Forrester and IDC are forecasting enormous growth in business-to-business trading over the internet, and those are the real published figures. Five real companies are on the table, each with its real financials and its name withheld. Students decide where the fund's money goes.

## Why it's hard

Most students accept the forecast, reasonably, since the direction turned out right. The harder question is who captures the value when the growth arrives. Before allocating a dollar, students state where they think the value will land and why, and that statement locks. They can hold cash, by naming where the value goes instead.

## What students take away

Getting the direction of a technology shift right is a different skill from knowing who profits from it. Students leave able to separate the two questions and argue the second with evidence. The reveal holds each locked statement up against what happened.

## Running it

You move the session along from the session bar: open the statements, open the fund, close it, then release 2002, 2004 and 2010; the company names come out at the end. Positions are valued the way a shareholder experienced them, through mergers and other events. Results show each fund against its own statement, and a facilitator note helps with the debrief. It fits a unit on IT and strategy, e-commerce or technology investing. If your students were right about the boom, where did they think the money would go?
