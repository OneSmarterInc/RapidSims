# RapidSim+ 01 · Why Don't They Have Any Patience?

About seventy minutes, extended format. Each student plays on their own device, and a run can span two class days.

## The situation

Wexford Benefit Administrators processes dental claims for employers' benefit plans and is replacing its claims system. The vendor needs a verified account of how claims arrive and reach the first review, and the chart on file is incomplete. Your students are the process analyst. They watch the review desk for ten minutes, then interview three employees, Ray, Terry and Ruth, in fixed fifteen-minute appointments.

## Why it's hard

Every question costs time, and broad questions cost the most. Each person knows part of the story, and how they answer depends on how they're asked; one question can make a source stop volunteering anything while staying perfectly polite. The cause sits between what two different people say, and students have to put it together.

## What students take away

This is a sim about process discovery and question formulation. Students learn to interview for how work really flows, to notice where it waits, and to back each recommendation with something they actually heard. They complete the process chart and file a four-step report the vendor will configure against.

## Running it

Students wait until you start the session, then each plays their own run; a run carries over to the next class day until you close the session. Results show each student's interviews, with the kind of each question and the moment Ruth opened up or stopped volunteering, plus their chart and report. The seven-slide debrief starts from the review desk and ends with four lessons drawn from your class. It fits a unit on systems analysis, business process management or requirements gathering. How many of your students would have asked to watch the work before asking about it?
