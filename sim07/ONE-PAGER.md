# RapidSim 07 · Would You Have Bought It?

A ten-minute decision clock, then a staged reveal and the debrief. Individual or team.

## The situation

It's autumn 2000, and your students run the largest video rental chain in the country. A small company that rents DVDs by mail has offered itself for fifty million dollars. It lost about thirty million on five million of revenue last year and pulled its stock offering in July. Students read the briefing, hear from four advisers, and decide to buy or decline.

## Why it's hard

Every figure comes from the companies' own filings, with the names withheld. The advisers split: two say decline, one says buy, one says wait a year, and each makes a case a sensible executive would accept. Students write a one-sentence reason before they commit, and the decision binds. Some will think they recognise the situation, and the sim asks them to decide on the evidence.

## What students take away

The sim separates what was knowable then from what hindsight makes obvious. Each student's reason stays pinned at the top of every reveal stage, word for word, as the years play out. The learning is in watching a reasonable argument age.

## Running it

You close the decisions from the session bar, then release 2002, 2005 and 2010 in order. In team play members vote privately and the majority decides. The Live screen shows how the class split, and the debrief puts two contrasting reasons side by side. It fits a strategy session, an introduction to information systems and business models, or any class on disruption. How many of your students would have bought it, and how many would say so before the reveal?
