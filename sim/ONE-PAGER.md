# RapidSim 01 · Disaster or Breach?

Twenty minutes of play, then the debrief. Each student plays on their own device.

## The situation

It's two in the morning at Northbeam Data Services, which runs the data platforms behind about forty enterprise clients: insurers, claims processors and a couple of regional banks. A claims report wouldn't reconcile just before midnight, and by one o'clock two more applications were throwing corruption errors that came straight back after a restart. Your students are the VP of Operations, and the incident call is already open.

## Why it's hard

A failing storage array and an intruder look the same at this hour. Four people are on the call, each competent and each with something to lose: the infrastructure director, the head of security, the storage vendor and the client lead with a board call at eight. Students question any of them, and the facts they need are spread across the call. Each person offers the part that helps them and keeps the part that hurts.

## What students take away

The sim is about sequencing under irreversibility. One move restores service fast; another keeps options open at a cost of hours. Students decide which matters more before they know what they're dealing with, and the debrief shows how the same move reads as decisive or reckless depending on a fact nobody could confirm at the time.

## Running it

Students lock a reading and an action at three points in the night, each with what would change their mind. Results show each student's three positions and where they ended up. You can hold each student's debrief until you release it. The class debrief runs from your screen and carries most of the teaching, so plan on an hour in total. It fits an operations, security or leadership session and works for any background. How would your students explain their call to the board at eight?
